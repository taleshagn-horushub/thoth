# Kishōtenketsu Framework for VSL Creation

## Overview

Kishōtenketsu (起承転結) is a four-act narrative structure originating from classical Chinese poetry. When applied to VSL (Video Sales Letter) creation, it creates compelling sales narratives that feel organic rather than manipulative.

## The Four Acts

### 1. KI (Introduction) - 0-15% of video

**Purpose:** Capture attention with an emotional, relatable story

**Characteristics:**
- Opens with a story seemingly unrelated to the product
- Creates immediate emotional connection
- Plants subtle "vestiges" (hints) about the solution
- Focuses on human experience, not sales pitch
- Builds curiosity and engagement

**Key Principles:**
- Start with a hook that's emotionally resonant
- Make it relatable to your target audience
- Don't mention the product yet
- Create a sense of "I need to know more"

**Red Flags:**
- Story feels forced or irrelevant
- Audience can see the sales angle immediately
- Too much information too quickly

### 2. SHO (Development) - 15-50% of video

**Purpose:** Connect the story to the customer's reality

**Characteristics:**
- Deepens the initial story
- Gradually reveals the problem
- Shows consequences of the problem
- Builds tension and relatability
- Prepares the ground for the solution

**Key Principles:**
- Maintain the narrative momentum
- Avoid using sales language (trust, promise, guarantee, etc.)
- Focus on the customer's pain, not the product
- Create a sense of "this is my situation"
- Build emotional investment

**Red Flags:**
- Mentioning the product too early
- Using sales/marketing jargon
- Losing the narrative thread
- Audience disengagement

### 3. TEN (Plot Twist) - 50-85% of video

**Purpose:** Reveal the solution and create desire

**Characteristics:**
- Unexpected shift that recontextualizes everything
- Presents the product as the obvious solution
- Creates the "wow" moment
- Generates desire without pressure
- Transforms perspective on the problem

**Key Principles:**
- Make the revelation impactful and surprising
- Connect the story directly to the solution
- Present the product with confidence (no discounts, no begging)
- Show the transformation that becomes possible
- Create a sense of "this is exactly what I needed"

**Red Flags:**
- Weak or predictable twist
- Product feels forced or disconnected
- Overselling or aggressive tactics
- Losing the elegance of the narrative

### 4. KETSU (Conclusion) - 85-100% of video

**Purpose:** Consolidate the decision and drive action

**Characteristics:**
- Reaffirms the offer from a new perspective
- Presents logistical details (guarantee, access, etc.)
- Shows confidence without desperation
- Provides clear call-to-action
- Ends with elegance and professionalism

**Key Principles:**
- Don't repeat the whole pitch
- Show confidence in the product
- Make the next step crystal clear
- Use strong guarantee only if justified
- End on a positive, professional note

**Red Flags:**
- Weak or unclear call-to-action
- Begging or desperate language
- Weak guarantee undermining confidence
- Losing the narrative quality

## Timing Guidelines

| Phase | Duration | Percentage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KI | 15-30 seconds | 15% |
| SHO | 60-120 seconds | 35% |
| TEN | 60-90 seconds | 35% |
| KETSU | 30-45 seconds | 15% |
| **Total** | **3-5 minutes** | **100%** |

## Kishōtenketsu vs. AIDA

| Aspect | Kishōtenketsu | AIDA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | 4 acts (narrative) | 4 stages (linear) |
| Approach | Indirect, story-driven | Direct, benefit-driven |
| Product mention | Late (TEN) | Early (Interest) |
| Psychology | "Wow, this solves it!" | "This makes sense" |
| Best for | High-ticket, cold traffic | Aware audiences, short ads |
| Aggressiveness | Low (organic) | High (persuasive) |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Weak Ki:** Story doesn't engage or feels irrelevant
2. **Premature Product Mention:** Mentioning product in Sho kills the narrative
3. **Predictable Ten:** Audience sees the twist coming
4. **Forced Connection:** Story doesn't connect logically to solution
5. **Weak Ketsu:** Unclear CTA or begging language
6. **Wrong Audience:** Using Kishōtenketsu for already-aware audiences
7. **Too Long:** Exceeding 5 minutes loses engagement

## When to Use Kishōtenketsu

✅ **Use Kishōtenketsu when:**
- Target audience is cold (low awareness)
- Product is high-ticket
- Offer is complex and requires belief shift
- Brand positioning is premium
- You want to stand out from aggressive competitors
- Audience is saturated with typical sales tactics

❌ **Don't use Kishōtenketsu when:**
- Audience is already aware and actively searching
- Product is low-ticket and impulse-driven
- You have very limited time (under 30 seconds)
- Audience expects direct, straightforward messaging
- Product is commodity-like with no unique angle

## Research Requirements

Before creating a Kishōtenketsu VSL, gather:

1. **Market Research:**
   - Market size and trends
   - Competitor positioning
   - Customer pain points
   - Market awareness level

2. **Customer Research:**
   - Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
   - Primary pain points
   - Current solutions they use
   - Objections and concerns
   - Desired outcomes

3. **Product Research:**
   - Unique mechanism/differentiator
   - Key benefits
   - Transformation it enables
   - Proof/social proof
   - Guarantee/security

4. **Narrative Elements:**
   - Relevant emotional stories
   - Customer success stories
   - Market insights
   - Unique perspectives
   - Transformation examples

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing your VSL, verify:

- [ ] Ki story is emotionally engaging and relatable
- [ ] Ki story feels relevant (subtle connection visible)
- [ ] Sho deepens the problem without mentioning product
- [ ] Sho uses no sales language
- [ ] Ten provides a genuine revelation/insight
- [ ] Ten connects story to solution logically
- [ ] Product is presented confidently in Ten
- [ ] Ketsu reaffirms without repeating
- [ ] CTA is clear and easy to follow
- [ ] Total duration is 3-5 minutes
- [ ] Pacing feels natural, not rushed
- [ ] Tone is professional but conversational
- [ ] No desperation or aggressive tactics
- [ ] Guarantee (if mentioned) is strong and justified

## Language-Specific Considerations

### Portuguese (Português)
- Use conversational, natural language
- Avoid overly formal or stiff phrasing
- Embrace storytelling traditions
- Use local references when appropriate
- Maintain warmth and authenticity

### English
- Use clear, direct language
- Avoid jargon unless audience-appropriate
- Maintain professional tone
- Use universal references
- Keep pacing crisp and engaging
